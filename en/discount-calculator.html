    Discount calculator

    Enter the original price and the percentage off to see what you pay and what you save. A second discount box handles “an extra 10% off the sale price” without the usual double-counting mistake.

    Runs locally in your browser

    How it works

    A discount of p percent leaves (100 − p) percent of the price, so the price after the discount is original × (1 − p ÷ 100). Two discounts are applied one after the other, never added: 20% then 10% leaves 0.8 × 0.9 = 0.72 of the original, which is 28% off, not 30%. The tool reports the effective total discount so the two figures can be compared. Prices are treated as plain numbers without a currency symbol.

    Worked example

    An 80 item with 25% off costs 80 × 0.75 = 60. A further 10% off that sale price takes it to 60 × 0.9 = 54, so you save 26 in total. That is 32.5% off the original, not the 35% you would get by adding the two percentages.

    Questions and answers

    Why is 20% then 10% not 30% off?

    Because the second discount applies to the reduced price, not the original one. 20% takes the price to 80% of the original, then 10% takes another tenth of that, leaving 72% — a 28% total discount.

    Can I use this for tax as well as discounts?

    You can, in the sense that the arithmetic is the same percentage change, but for adding VAT use the VAT calculator, which labels net, tax and gross properly and works in both directions.

    What if the discount is exactly 100%?

    Then the final price is zero and the saving is the whole original price. The tool accepts 100% and reports the result rather than treating it as an error.

    Does it handle prices with decimals?

    Yes. Type 19.99 and it is treated as 19.99. Commas as thousand separators are also accepted, so 1,200 and 1200 both work.

    Why does it not show a currency symbol?

    Because the tool does not know which currency you are shopping in. The numbers you get are in whatever unit you typed, so the result is exact for any currency.
