    Time zone converter

    Pick a date, a time and a reference zone, then add the zones you want to compare. Each row shows the local time there, its UTC offset and whether daylight saving is in force.

    Runs locally in your browser

    How it works

    The converter takes your date and time in the reference zone, turns it into one moment in time, and formats that moment in every zone you add. Each offset comes from Intl.DateTimeFormat, so it follows the time-zone database built into your browser rather than a table written here. Because that database knows the daylight-saving rules, an offset changes with the date: New York is UTC−5 in January and UTC−4 in July. The UTC offset printed next to each result is the amount added to UTC at that moment. A zone is marked as observing daylight saving when its current offset is larger than the smaller of its January and July offsets. Zones are named by their IANA identifiers, such as Europe/London. Results depend on the database version your browser ships, and nothing is sent anywhere.

    Worked example

    12:00 on 8 March 2026 in UTC gives 8:00 AM in New York (EDT, UTC−4, because daylight saving started that morning), 12:00 PM in London (GMT, UTC+0) and 9:00 PM in Tokyo (UTC+9). The same date in January would give 7:00 AM in New York, because the offset there is UTC−5 then.

    Questions and answers

    Which time zones can I choose?

    The picker lists every zone your browser reports, which is the IANA set: names such as Europe/Berlin, America/Sao_Paulo and Asia/Tokyo. UTC is always in the list. The reference zone defaults to the one your browser is set to.

    Does it handle daylight saving?

    Yes, because the offset is read for the exact date you choose, not fixed per zone. The 8 March example above is a daylight-saving change: the same clock time in New York is four hours behind UTC that day and five hours behind in January.

    Why might this differ from a clock in that city?

    Two reasons. Your browser's time-zone database has a version, and a government can change its rules after that version shipped. And a city can choose not to follow the zone that carries its name. The identifier in each row tells you which zone was used.

    What does UTC−04:00 mean?

    It means the local clock is four hours behind Coordinated Universal Time. To get UTC, add four hours to the local time. A positive offset such as UTC+09:00 means the local clock is ahead of UTC.

    Is the date or the zone list sent anywhere?

    No. The conversion runs with the browser's Intl API in this tab. There is no request, no account and no stored history, so reloading the page clears everything.
