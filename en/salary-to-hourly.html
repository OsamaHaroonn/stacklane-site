    Salary to hourly calculator

    Enter a yearly salary plus the hours and weeks you work, and the tool divides it down to an hourly rate with the daily, weekly and monthly equivalents.

    Runs locally in your browser

    How it works

    The hourly rate is the yearly salary divided by the paid hours in a year, and those hours are your hours per week multiplied by your weeks per year. Forty hours over fifty-two weeks is 2,080 hours, so a 52,000 salary gives 25.00 an hour. The weekly figure divides the salary by the number of weeks, the monthly figure divides it by twelve, and the daily figure takes the hourly rate and multiplies it by the weekly hours divided by five. Every result is printed with the arithmetic that produced it, so you can check each line yourself. No tax, pension or holiday rules are applied: the tool only divides the number you type.

    Worked example

    For a 52,000 salary at 40 hours a week over 52 weeks, the paid hours are 40 × 52 = 2,080. The hourly rate is 52,000 ÷ 2,080 = 25.00. The daily figure is 25.00 × (40 ÷ 5) = 200.00, the weekly figure is 52,000 ÷ 52 = 1,000.00, and the monthly figure is 52,000 ÷ 12 = 4,333.33.

    Questions and answers

    Does this include tax?

    No. It is arithmetic only: no income tax, national insurance, pension or student loan is subtracted. Type the gross salary to compare job offers, or a net figure if you already know your take-home pay.

    Why divide by hours per week times weeks per year?

    Because that product is the number of paid hours in a year. At 40 hours over 52 weeks it is 2,080 hours, so dividing the salary by 2,080 gives the rate for each paid hour.

    Should I use 52 weeks if I get paid holiday?

    Use the number of weeks you are actually paid for. If your salary is spread across the full year including holiday, keep 52. If you are paid only for weeks worked, enter the smaller number.

    How is the daily figure worked out?

    The hourly rate is multiplied by your weekly hours divided by five. That assumes five working days sharing the same weekly hours, so it is an average, not a timesheet.

    What if my hours change from week to week?

    Enter an average week. The result is a straight division, so it can only be as accurate as that average.
