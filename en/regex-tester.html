    Developer

    Regex tester

    Enter a pattern, choose flags and paste the text to search. The tool lists each match with its position and capture groups, and explains any pattern that will not compile.

    Runs locally in your browser

    How it works

    The pattern is compiled with the RegExp constructor, which is the same engine your browser runs at run time, not a lookalike written for this page. Flags are passed as written: g finds every match, i ignores case, m makes ^ and $ match line ends, s lets a dot match newlines and u enables Unicode mode. Each match is reported with its start and end index plus its text. Capture groups are numbered in the order their opening bracket appears, and named groups are shown under their name. The tool stops after 1,000 matches so a broad pattern cannot hang the tab, and it refuses patterns that nest a quantifier inside a quantified group, such as (a+)+, because those can backtrack exponentially.

    Worked example

    With the pattern \b(\w+)-(\d+)\b and the g flag, the text "build-42 and patch-7" gives two matches. Match 1 is build-42 at indices 0 to 8, with group 1 build and group 2 42. Match 2 is patch-7 at indices 13 to 20, with groups patch and 7.

    Questions and answers

    Which regular-expression flavour is this?

    JavaScript, exactly as your browser implements it. That includes named groups, lookahead, lookbehind and Unicode property escapes in current browsers. PCRE-only syntax such as recursion or branch-reset groups is not available.

    Why was my pattern refused?

    Two guards. A pattern longer than 300 characters is refused, and so is one that puts a quantifier inside an already quantified group, because that shape can take exponential time on a hostile input. The message names the reason.

    Do matches update while I type?

    Yes. Every change to the pattern, the flags or the text re-runs the match and replaces the list. Invalid patterns show the engine's error message instead of matches.

    Why does ^ not match the start of every line?

    Without the m flag, ^ and $ mean the start and end of the whole string. Turn on m to make them match around line breaks, which is what most multi-line text needs.

    Is the text I paste uploaded?

    No. Matching happens in this tab and nothing is sent. There is no history, so the pattern and text disappear when you reload.
