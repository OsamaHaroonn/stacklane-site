    Percentage calculator

    Three common percentage problems in one page: percentage of a number, one number as a percentage of another, and increase or decrease by a percentage. Each answer shows the working.

    Runs locally in your browser

    How it works

    Percentage of a number multiplies the value by the percentage and divides by 100. “X is what percent of Y” divides X by Y and multiplies by 100, and is refused when Y is zero because division by zero has no answer. Increase and decrease apply the percentage to the original value first, then add or subtract it, so a 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease lands 4% below where you started — the two percentages do not cancel each other out.

    Worked example

    15% of 240 = 0.15 × 240 = 36. The number 36 is 15% of 240. Increasing 240 by 15% gives 276; decreasing 276 by 15% gives 234.60 — not back at 240, which is the trap this page shows in full.

    Questions and answers

    Why can't I divide by zero?

    Because there is no number that multiplied by zero gives a nonzero value. When the base is zero the tool says so instead of showing infinity or an error.

    What is the difference between the three modes?

    “% of” gives a part of a number. “X is what %” compares two numbers as a percentage. “Increase/decrease” applies a percentage change to a starting value.

    Are the results rounded?

    The exact result is shown to two decimal places, and the working above each answer shows the unrounded value so you can round the way you need.

    Does a 50% increase and a 50% decrease cancel out?

    No. Going up 50% and then down 50% from the new, larger number lands you 25% below where you started. The examples on this page show why.

    Is anything stored?

    No. Every calculation is done in your browser and disappears when you clear the fields or close the page.
