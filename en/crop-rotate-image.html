Crop and rotate image

Choose a picture, set the rectangle you want to keep in pixels, pick an angle and download the result with its exact new size.

Runs locally in your browser

How it works

The crop rectangle is given in pixels measured from the top-left corner of the original image, and the region inside it is copied onto a canvas. The rotation is applied afterwards to that cut-out, so the numbers you typed never move when you change the angle. Turning by 90 or 270 degrees swaps the width and height of the result; 180 leaves them as they are. The output keeps the source format where the browser can produce it — JPG, PNG or WebP — and otherwise falls back to PNG, with white painted behind the picture for JPG because JPEG has no alpha channel. A rectangle that runs past the edge of the image, a zero-sized side or a non-whole number is refused before anything is drawn.

Worked example

An 800 × 600 photo cropped at x 100, y 50 with 400 × 300 fits: 100 + 400 = 500 is inside 800, and 50 + 300 = 350 is inside 600. The cut-out is 400 × 300, and rotating it 90 degrees swaps the sides, so the result row reads 300 × 400 px. Rotating again runs the same crop from the original image, so the rectangle stays 400 × 300 every time instead of shrinking.
