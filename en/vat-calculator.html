    VAT calculator

    Type the amount and the VAT rate that applies to you, and choose whether to add VAT to a net price or remove it from a price that already includes it.

    Runs locally in your browser

    How it works

    To add VAT, the amount is the net figure, the tax is net × rate ÷ 100, and the gross is the two added together. To remove VAT, the amount you typed is the gross, so the net is gross ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100) and the tax is the difference. Removing VAT is not the same as subtracting the rate: taking 20% off a gross figure over-refunds, because the 20% was charged on the smaller net figure. The rate is deliberately not pre-filled, because it differs by country and by item, and it changes.

    Worked example

    At 20%: a net price of 200 has VAT of 200 × 20 ÷ 100 = 40 and a gross of 240. Going the other way, a gross of 240 gives a net of 240 ÷ 1.2 = 200 and VAT of 40 — subtracting 20% of 240 would wrongly give 192.

    Questions and answers

    Why is the rate blank?

    Because there is no single correct rate. Countries, regions and product categories all differ, and rates change. Typing the rate yourself keeps the tool honest instead of guessing a jurisdiction for you.

    Why is removing VAT not just minus the percentage?

    The percentage was charged on the net amount, so it is a smaller share of the gross. Dividing by (1 + rate ÷ 100) reverses the calculation exactly; subtracting the rate does not.

    Can I use a 0% rate?

    Yes. A zero rate leaves the net and gross equal and the tax at zero, which is what a zero-rated or exempt supply looks like arithmetically.

    Does this work for sales tax and GST?

    The arithmetic is the same for any percentage-based tax added to a net price. Only the label differs, so you can use it for GST, sales tax or a service tax by typing that rate.

    Is the result rounded the way an invoice must be?

    No. The tool shows two decimals for reading, but tax authorities have their own rounding rules — often per line or per invoice. Round the final tax figure the way your jurisdiction requires.
