    Mortgage calculator

    Enter the property price, your down payment, the annual rate and the term to see the monthly principal-and-interest payment, the loan size and the interest over the full term.

    Runs locally in your browser

    How it works

    The loan is the price minus the down payment. The monthly payment then comes from M = L × i ÷ (1 − (1 + i)^−n), with i the annual rate divided by 100 and then by 12, and n the number of monthly payments. The tool also reports the down payment as a percentage of the price and the total cost including the deposit. Property tax, buildings insurance, mortgage insurance, arrangement fees and service charges are not part of this figure, and a variable rate will change it.

    Worked example

    A 300,000 home with a 60,000 down payment leaves a 240,000 loan. At 6% over 30 years, i = 0.005 and n = 360, so the payment is 240,000 × 0.005 ÷ (1 − 1.005^−360) = 1,438.92. The down payment is 20% of the price, 360 payments total 518,011.20 and the interest is 278,011.20.

    Questions and answers

    Does this include tax and insurance?

    No. It is principal and interest only. Many lenders collect property tax and insurance alongside the payment, which makes the amount you actually pay higher than the figure shown here.

    What down payment should I enter?

    The amount you pay up front in cash. If you are still deciding, try a few values: a larger deposit lowers both the loan and the interest, while the same deposit on a longer term lowers the payment but raises the total interest.

    Why is the total cost bigger than the price?

    The tool adds the down payment to every monthly payment. Interest across a 25- or 30-year term is a large share of that sum, which is why the total can approach or pass double the loan.

    What about a variable or fixed rate?

    The maths treats the rate you enter as fixed for the whole term. With a variable rate, re-run it at the rate you expect after each change to see the effect.

    Is this a mortgage offer?

    No. It is an estimate from four numbers you typed. Lenders apply affordability checks, fees and their own criteria, and the rate you are offered may differ.
