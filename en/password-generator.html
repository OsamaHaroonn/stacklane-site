    Generators

    Password generator

    Choose a length and which character sets to use, and generate a random password. It is built from your browser's random number generator and never leaves the page.

    Runs locally in your browser

    How it works

    Each character is drawn uniformly at random from the alphabet you enable, using the browser's cryptographically secure random source. Strength is shown as entropy in bits: length multiplied by the log₂ of the alphabet size. Eight bits roughly means one chance in 256 of guessing, so 60 bits and above is strong for everyday accounts and 100 bits and above is strong even against patient, well-resourced guessing.

    Worked example

    16 characters drawn from upper case, lower case, digits and symbols gives an alphabet of 94 characters: 16 × log₂(94) ≈ 105 bits. Reducing it to 8 characters drops the same alphabet to about 52 bits — half the strength, and much easier to guess by brute force.

    Questions and answers

    Where do these passwords come from?

    From your browser's cryptographic random generator (crypto.getRandomValues). Nothing is requested from a server, so no third party sees the password.

    How strong is strong enough?

    As a rough guide, 40 bits is weak, 60–80 bits is good for most accounts, and 100 bits or more resists serious offline guessing. The tool shows the bits it calculated for your settings.

    Should I use a different password everywhere?

    Yes. Reusing a password means one breached site exposes every other account. Store unique passwords in a password manager rather than memorising them.

    Why is the password not remembered after I refresh?

    Deliberately. Nothing is stored in cookies, local storage or on a server, so a refresh simply generates a new one. Save it where you keep your other passwords.

    Can it make memorable passphrases instead?

    Not yet. This tool produces random character strings. A passphrase of random words is also a good choice, but this page does not generate one.
