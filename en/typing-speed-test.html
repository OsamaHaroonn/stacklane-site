    Text

    Typing speed test

    Type the sample text below. The clock starts on your first keystroke and stops when you press Finish; you get words per minute, accuracy and the arithmetic behind both.

    Runs locally in your browser

    How it works

    Words per minute here uses the five-character convention: WPM = correct characters ÷ 5 ÷ minutes elapsed. A character counts as correct when your text matches the sample at that position, so a typo costs one character even if the rest lines up. Accuracy = correct characters ÷ characters typed × 100. The clock uses the browser's high-resolution timer and starts on the first keystroke rather than on a button, so the result includes the time spent reading the sample. Nothing is recorded: no history, no cookies, no local storage, so reloading the page clears the attempt. The samples are built in and short enough to finish in well under a minute at an ordinary speed.

    Worked example

    Typing 250 characters in exactly one minute with 25 of them wrong leaves 225 correct. That is 225 ÷ 5 ÷ 1 = 45 words per minute, with 225 ÷ 250 = 90% accuracy. The same 225 characters typed in 30 seconds would read as 90 WPM, because half the time means twice the rate.

    Questions and answers

    Why five characters per word?

    It is the long-standing typing convention: it treats a word plus the space after it as five characters, so spaces and punctuation count. A five-letter word typed with a trailing space is one word for this measurement.

    Does fixing a typo count as correct?

    Yes. The comparison uses the final text in the box, position by position, so a corrected character counts as correct. The time you spent fixing it still counts against you, which is why accuracy and speed are reported separately.

    What if I stop before the end?

    Finish uses whatever you have typed. Accuracy then compares against only those characters, and WPM counts the correct ones. An attempt shorter than a sentence is not a useful measurement.

    Are my results stored or compared with anyone?

    No. There is no account, no leaderboard and no storage of any kind. The numbers exist in the page until you reload it or type again.

    How accurate is a very short attempt?

    Short attempts are noisy because the rate is extrapolated to a full minute. A few seconds of typing can read as several hundred WPM. Type for at least 20 to 30 seconds if you want a figure that means something.
