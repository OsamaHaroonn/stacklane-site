Coin flipper

Flip a coin as many times as you like and watch the heads and tails counts build up, with the share of each side shown as it goes.

Runs locally in your browser

How it works

Each flip asks the browser for 32 random bits and reduces that number to one of two outcomes. A plain remainder would favour one side slightly, because 2 does not divide the range of a 32-bit number, so values above the largest exact multiple of two are thrown away and drawn again. Every accepted value is therefore mapped to heads or tails with exactly equal chance, and every flip is independent of the ones before it. The counters live in this page only: they start at zero, change as you flip, and go back to zero when you press reset or reload. The percentages are each count divided by the total flips so far.

Worked example

If the first four flips came out heads, tails, heads, heads, the counter would read heads 3, which is 3 ÷ 4 × 100 = 75.0%, tails 1, which is 25.0%, and a total of 4. Each flip is independent, so that 75% is just what happened in those four flips, not a prediction for the next one.
