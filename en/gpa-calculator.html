    GPA calculator

    Enter each course with its grade points and its units, and the tool returns the weighted average, the total units and the quality points behind it.

    Runs locally in your browser

    How it works

    GPA = total quality points ÷ total units. Quality points for one course are its grade points multiplied by its units, so a course worth four units counts twice as much as one worth two — this is why a high mark in a large course moves the average more than a high mark in a small one. The tool adds up the quality points and the units across every row you fill in and divides one by the other. The grade scale is entirely yours: there is no built-in scale, because scales differ between countries, institutions and departments, and claiming one standard would be wrong. Rows left blank are ignored, so you can keep a spare row ready.

    Worked example

    Three courses: A with 4.0 points for 3 units, B with 3.0 points for 4 units, and A with 4.0 points for 2 units. The quality points are 4.0 × 3 = 12, 3.0 × 4 = 12 and 4.0 × 2 = 8, which add up to 32, and the units are 3 + 4 + 2 = 9. The GPA is 32 ÷ 9 = 3.56.

    Questions and answers

    Which grade scale does this use?

    The one you type in. Each row takes the grade points your institution assigns, so a 4.0 scale, a 5.0 scale, a 10-point scale or anything else works as long as you enter the points.

    What is the difference between grade points and units?

    Grade points are the value of the mark itself, such as 4.0 for an A. Units (credits, hours) are the weight of the course. The average is weighted by units, not a plain mean of the marks.

    Do I need to fill in the course name?

    No. The name is only there to help you keep track of which row is which; the calculation uses the points and units alone.

    Why is my GPA lower than the plain average of my marks?

    Because it is weighted. If your weakest marks sit in your highest-unit courses, they pull the weighted average down further than a simple average would.

    Does this match my institution's official GPA?

    It matches the arithmetic, but institutions round differently, drop or repeat modules, and may use their own scale conversions. Check your own rules before relying on the figure.
