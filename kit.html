Pack 01

Quote templates — three structures

Pick one. Never send a bare number in an email body — an unstructured price looks like a guess.

A · One-page quote (simple jobs, under $2,000)

[Business name] — Quote #[001] Prepared for: [Client, business] Date: [date] · Valid for: 14 days The outcome [One sentence in the client's language: "A live booking page so customers can book you at 11pm without calling."] What's included • [Deliverable 1] • [Deliverable 2] • [Deliverable 3] What's not included [Name 2 things. This line prevents 90% of arguments later.] Price: $[amount] — fixed Deposit: 50% to start, 50% on delivery. Delivery: [date].

B · Itemised quote (clients who want to see the maths)

[Business name] — Quote #[001] for [Client] Discovery & setup $[ ] Build / production $[ ] Testing & handover $[ ] Contingency (10%) $[ ] Total: $[amount] Payment: 50% deposit, balance within 7 days of delivery. Changes outside this list are quoted separately before any work starts.

C · Scope-based quote (highest value — sell the outcome, not the hours)

Proposal: [Outcome, not deliverable] The problem today [What is happening now and what it costs them — a number if you have one.] What you'll have [The end state, in their words.] How it gets done Week 1: [step] · Week 2: [step] · Week 3: [step] Investment: $[amount] Less than [what one lost customer / one lost week costs them]. Deposit 50% to start. Preview and approval before final payment. Guarantee If the preview doesn't match what we agreed, you don't pay the balance.

Five lines that protect your price:

1. "This is a fixed price for the scope above — not an hourly estimate." 2. "Anything outside this list gets a separate quote first. Nothing gets added silently." 3. "The price holds for 14 days; after that I re-check availability." 4. "50% starts the work, 50% releases it. I don't finance projects." 5. "[The outcome] typically pays for itself within [period] — if it doesn't, we should talk before you pay the balance."
Pack 02

Invoice & payment terms pack

Use the free invoice generator to produce the numbers — these are the words around them.

Deposit invoice (start of project)

Deposit invoice #[001] Project: [name] for [Client] Amount due to start: $[amount] (50% of $[total]) Work begins on receipt. The remaining balance is invoiced on delivery and is due within 7 days. Thank you.

Stage invoice (multi-week projects)

Stage invoice #[003] — [stage name] Completed: [what is finished, concretely] This stage: $[amount] · Total invoiced to date: $[x] of $[total] See what's ready for review

Final invoice (release on handover)

Final invoice #[005] — [Project] Balance due: $[amount] Delivered: [date] · [List what was handed over: files, access, training] Payment terms: 7 days. Late payments carry 1.5% per month. Access to [hosting/account] is transferred once payment clears.

Late payment language — send on day 15, not day 60

Polite reminder (day 15) Hi [name], invoice #[001] for $[amount] was due [date]. Could you let me know when it's scheduled? Happy to resend the details if they're buried. Firm (day 30) Hi [name], this invoice is now [n] days past due. I'd rather sort it out than escalate. Please confirm a payment date by [date this week]. Final (day 45) Hi [name], invoice #[001] ($[amount]) is [n] days overdue. Work on [project] pauses from today, and I'll add 1.5% monthly late fee per our terms. Pay by [date] and everything resumes the same day.

Put these three lines at the bottom of every invoice: Payment terms: [7/14] days · Late fee: 1.5% monthly · Deposits are non-refundable once work begins.

Pack 04

The pricing sheet

Cost-plus (your floor — never quote below this) price = (hours × your rate + materials + overhead) × (1 + margin) Value-based (what you should quote) value = what the result is worth to them over a year quote ≈ 10–30% of that value Worked example 8 hours × $40 = $320 · materials $150 · overhead $60 break-even = $530 · +25% margin = $662.50 If it saves them 6 hours a week, that's ~$1,250/month of their time. Value-based quote would be $600–$2,000 — so quote the value, not the floor.

Run the numbers in the free pricing calculator instead of by hand.

Three rules that protect you 1. Never quote under your floor. Add scope instead, or decline. 2. Quote three tiers: Essential / Standard / Recommended — most buyers take the middle. Show it as "Recommended". 3. Round up. $1,470 looks like you added up wrong; $1,500 looks like a business. Anchor line for every proposal "Comparable work is typically $[market high]. I'm at $[your price] because [scope is tight / you're building a portfolio / no agency overhead]."
Pack 05

Discovery call — 9 questions

Ask all nine before you quote. The first three tell you if there's a real problem; the last three tell you if there's a deal.

1. What does the business do, and who exactly buys it? 2. What have you already tried — and what happened? 3. What is this costing you now? (hours per week, missed jobs, lost customers) 4. How do customers find you and book you today? Walk me through the last one. 5. What does "fixed" look like in 90 days? Give me the number or the outcome. 6. Who else has a say in saying yes? 7. What budget range were you expecting? (Ask it flat, then stop talking.) 8. Is there a date this must be live by? 9. If nothing changes, what happens in six months?
Red flags — end the call politely • Answers #3 with "I don't know" → no awareness of pain, long sale ahead. • Answer #7: "as cheap as possible" → price buyer, not value buyer. • Answer #9: "nothing really" → the project isn't urgent. Park it. • They want to pay after delivery → say no: "50% starts it. That's the term." • Three people must approve → expect a slow, watered-down project.
Pack 06

Scope-creep response cards

Say them warmly and immediately. Delay is what turns a favour into an expectation.

"Can you just quickly…" "I can — that sits outside this scope though. It's [$x] / [x hours]. Want me to add it to this invoice or quote it as a separate job?" "While you're in there…" "Happy to. I'll quote it before I touch it so nothing surprises you at the end. Expect it by [time]." "The other agency said they'd include it free" "That may well be — worth checking what's excluded on their side. My price covers [scope]; I'd rather be clear than cheap and renegotiate later." "We need it changed, not extra — just different" "Two rounds of changes are already in the price, so this one's covered. After that it's [$x] per round. Want me to keep going?" "Can we add one more page/feature?" "Yes: [$x] added, delivery moves to [date]. Confirm and I'll start today." "It's urgent, can you drop everything?" "I can prioritise it — that costs 25% rush on top. Otherwise it starts [date]. Which do you want?"
The line that saves every project "Everything outside the quote gets a price before it gets my time. Nothing gets done behind your back and billed later."
